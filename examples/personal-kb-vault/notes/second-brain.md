# Building a second brain

The phrase that stuck with me: *your brain is for having ideas, not holding
them.* A second brain is just a trusted place outside my head where ideas live,
connect, and wait for me to come back. For me that place is this vault — plain
Markdown files I own.

## What actually makes it work

It's tempting to think the magic is in capture, or in tags, or in some clever
folder structure. It isn't. After a lot of false starts, three things matter:

1. **Capture has to be frictionless.** If saving an idea takes more than a few
   seconds, I won't do it. So I capture into a single [inbox](../inbox.md), from
   the editor or straight from the terminal, and sort later.
2. **Notes have to link.** A pile of isolated notes is a graveyard. Value comes
   from the connections between ideas, not the ideas in isolation. Links beat
   tags for me.
3. **Review is the whole game.** Capture is easy; *coming back* is the
   discipline. The technique I lean on for that is
   [spaced repetition](spaced-repetition.md) — revisit a note right before I'd
   otherwise forget it.

## How I run it here

- Everything starts in the [inbox](../inbox.md).
- The keepers become **evergreen notes** in this folder — one idea per note,
  rewritten in my own words, refined whenever I touch them again.
- Hub pages — **maps of content** like [reading](../moc/reading.md) — link
  related notes together so nothing gets buried.
- Daily notes (see [today](../daily/2026-06-14.md)) are the working log; the
  evergreen notes are what survives.
